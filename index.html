About Me

I am a doctoral researcher in mathematical statistics at the Technical University of Munich, supervised by Mathias Drton. I started my PhD in January 2026.

My research connects causal inference, graphical models and stochastic dynamics. I am particularly interested in stationary diffusions: stochastic differential equations whose stationary distributions can model observations at equilibrium. Their dynamics allow feedback between variables, while the modeled distribution remains time-invariant. This is what draws me to them: a way to study causal cycles through the dynamics underlying equilibrium observations. Cycles have fascinated me since I first encountered causal inference.

My PhD is funded by the TUM Georg Nemetschek Institute through ThermoStat, a project connecting causal modeling with human thermal physiology and thermal comfort in buildings.

I studied mathematics in Bonn, where I fell in love with graph theory, and later completed my master’s at TUM. During that time, reading The Book of Why sparked my interest in causality. Much of what keeps me in research is the pleasure of an unexpected connection or an idea suddenly making sense. Alongside that book, Kosaraju’s algorithm and Stein discrepancies belong to my personal collection of “aha” moments.

Outside research, I enjoy hiking, cycling and skiing, preferably in the mountains. I am also curious about ’pataphysics—something I would like to find more time to explore.
