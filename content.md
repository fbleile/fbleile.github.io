## About Me

I am a doctoral researcher in mathematical statistics at the Technical University of Munich, supervised by [Mathias Drton](https://www.professoren.tum.de/en/drton-mathias). I started my PhD in January 2026.

My research connects causal inference, graphical models and stochastic dynamics. I am particularly interested in stationary diffusions: stochastic differential equations whose stationary distributions can model observations at equilibrium. Their dynamics allow feedback between variables, while the modeled distribution remains time-invariant. This is what draws me to them: a way to study causal cycles through the dynamics underlying equilibrium observations. Cycles have fascinated me since I first encountered causal inference.

My PhD is funded by the [TUM Georg Nemetschek Institute](https://www.mdsi.tum.de/gni/startseite/) through [ThermoStat](https://www.mdsi.tum.de/gni/gni-funded-projects/thermostat/), a project connecting causal modeling with human thermal physiology and thermal comfort in buildings.

I studied mathematics in Bonn, where I fell in love with graph theory, and later completed my master’s at TUM. During that time, reading [*The Book of Why*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Book_of_Why) sparked my interest in causality. Much of what keeps me in research is the pleasure of an unexpected connection or an idea suddenly making sense. Alongside that book, [Kosaraju’s algorithm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosaraju%27s_algorithm) and [Stein discrepancies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stein_discrepancy) belong to my personal collection of “aha” moments.

Outside research, I enjoy hiking, cycling and skiing, preferably in the mountains. I am also curious about [’pataphysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%27Pataphysics)—something I would like to find more time to explore.
