An explorable introduction to stationary diffusions

The Duck River

One duck is hard to predict. A whole river of ducks develops a pattern. Scroll the story on the left while the waterpark remains alive on the right.

01 / Follow one duck

Start with a single duck. It is carried clockwise by a current, but its path is never perfectly smooth. The faint trail is its recent history: a little memory of a random trajectory.

There is no wall. The glowing river is a preferred region, and a soft force pushes the duck back toward the water whenever noise carries it away.

02 / The river has a field

The image is not only decoration. Its pixels define the force field. Bright water attracts from the inside and outside; the tangent to the ring carries the duck around it.

drift = tangential flow + pixel attraction

03 / Add more ducks

Press spawn duck. Each new duck follows the same field but receives its own random kicks. The right-hand plot adds one small glow for every duck and shows where the population is now.

At a glance, the individual paths look noisy. Across many ducks, the river’s structure becomes visible.

04 / A still pattern in moving water

After enough time, the distribution of ducks can settle even though no duck stops. This is the first intuition for a stationary diffusion: motion at the particle level, stability at the population level.
